The six game manifests refine the existing guide records into focused, reviewed facts and directed relationships. They supplement the original source engine; they do not replace saved progress, edition checks or source attribution. A classifier proposes the requested relationship. It never supplies an answer fact.

Each manifest has `schemaVersion: 1`, `gameId`, a review date, `facts`, `entities` and `links`. A fact has a stable `id`, `subject`, descriptive `facet`, retrieval `aliases`, exact `text` and `witness`, and a `source` with file, locator and review. Guide excerpts must have `text === witness` and occur inside an authorized existing `sourceRecordId`. A more precise step locator may refine that record's source locator. Topic labels are retrieval aids, not declarations that every label is the same entity.

Every fact also declares reviewed `answerRelations`: the actual relationships its witness can answer. A location does not imply a route, acquisition does not imply use, a bare objective does not describe a procedure, and mentioning a person does not define that person. Descriptive facet labels and MiniLM similarity cannot grant a missing capability. Person/item/place kinds and directed endpoint roles remain distinct.

`answerBindings` scopes a witness to a particular mentioned entity and its supported roles. Each binding names a declared `entityId`, a nonempty subset of the fact's `answerRelations`, and a literal `mention` from the witness that matches the entity's name or alias. This lets a crafting passage answer about the basket or its seller without treating every mentioned person as its maker. When a binding applies, its roles take precedence over the passage's general roles for that entity.

`topicLabels` names descriptive facets such as “HMS Cape speed effect” or “training points per level”. Labels contain at least two words and cannot redefine a concrete person's, item's or place's name. Exact “what is”, “describe” and “explain” requests can use a reviewed label, while a person's identity still needs identity evidence. These labels do not grant a missing procedure or override source and edition restrictions.

An external research fact needs explicit `external-research` provenance, labeled primary references, a checked date and a reviewed research witness. Its authored summary remains distinct from the quoted witness and from the guide. Conflicting claims are retained as conflicts rather than promoted to certainty.

Entities have `id`, `name`, `kind` and optional aliases. Links have `id`, `from`, `to`, `relation`, `evidenceFactIds`, `status` and qualifiers. Their direction and conditions require human/agent source review: co-occurrence never creates an edge, and walkthrough ordering never establishes a prerequisite. The runtime only answers from the cited evidence; graph traversal does not invent transitive facts.

A fact may declare one level of `support`, keyed by requested relationship, with up to three other fact IDs. This is an authored composition of evidence, such as a dungeon's particular Word of Power plus the source's explanation of how Words unlock entrances. The runtime does not infer this relation from nearby text, recursively follow support, or infer that the player completed either step. Each component keeps its own source and conditions. `listMembers` can produce a names-only list when every name occurs in the witness; this avoids repeating irrelevant disputed attributions from a larger source paragraph.

The six production factories load their own manifest and static MiniLM fact vectors. They reuse the approved local encoder and classifier; there are no additional weights or package installations. The query vector is computed locally after existing chat consent. It ranks source passages; the answer still comes from authorized guide facts, reviewed research, explicit relationships and their qualifiers. Static vector catalogs must match every fact ID/document and the exact pinned encoder revision. Query vectors are kept only in bounded worker memory during normal use.

The original source engine continues to own progress, actions, choices and other gameplay state. Focused facets do not become arbitrary new facts in its progress model. Person/item/place salience comes from reviewed directed links; unknown subjects invalidate it. Source-only requests, editions, unsupported relationships and the distinction between an objective and an actual procedure remain part of answer authorization.

Source answers may carry a recorded person and item separately through a follow-up, including an explicitly recorded parent relationship. This records conversational references, not ownership or completed actions. Questions such as “So, has my party already banished him?” return explicit uncertainty about the saved game and write no progress. An unambiguous named subject in the same message can resolve a pronoun; two unrelated possible antecedents remain ambiguous. A recorded assembly relationship can keep the assembled object in scope when a follow-up asks about assembling one of its parts.

Bounded compound source questions run each clause with its own exact query encoding and shared source/edition/context restrictions. Every clause must return complete authorized evidence; otherwise the response declines and names the unanswered part. Opaque router checkpoints roll back provisional topic references after partial failure, cancellation or context changes. Reports, saved-game questions and conditional progress still use their original guarded paths. Possessive components such as “the Pistol and its canisters” remain one coordinated object; explicit properties such as “and its cost?” remain a second request.

`knowledge-graph.js` reads a finite set of authored direct predicate directions for makers, providers, teachers, repairers, requirements, locations and recorded literal answers. It preserves every required proof and withholds an incomplete or disputed match. The parent applies the same material/source/edition constraints to graph and passage answers. A location answer keeps its item as the conversational topic; a subsequent “there” can refer to the place while “it/them” continues to refer to the item. Unsupported relationships on a known subject retain that subject; an unknown name invalidates it.

Per-game fixed conversation cases contain dialogues and turns with original question text and independently source-checked expectations. `factIds` is a one-of set; `allFactIds` requires every listed fact. `contains`/`notContains` are case-insensitive source phrase checks. `not-answer` prevents unsupported answers. Source, relationship, uncertainty and context transitions matter alongside phrase matching. Game audits document coverage and remaining gaps; a passing corpus is not a claim to know every fact about a game.

`alternativeAnswers` defines complete independently reviewed alternatives, each with its own source and required phrases; evidence from different branches cannot be mixed. `sourceRecordIds` lists possible provenance witnesses; `allSourceRecordIds` and `allSources` require every listed witness/source. Equivalence is claim-specific: identical item-location text does not make two different items interchangeable.

Reproduce fact vectors with `python3 guides/tests/assistant/build-knowledge-embeddings.py --include-queries`; verify them without inference with `--verify-only`. The generator uses the already available local Chrome, Playwright and pinned model files, refuses external traffic, and does not install or download dependencies. The optional query-vector output is an ignored replay aid, not a shipped model or a training set.

`guides/tests/assistant/knowledge-corpus-replay.mjs` checks every fixed dialogue against the packaged classifier and exact cached MiniLM inputs, including context-expanded queries. Missing vectors, changed sources and failed assertions prevent acceptance. `guides/tests/assistant/knowledge-browser.py` runs the actual production chat with native classification/encoding and checks rendered source citations. It explicitly cannot observe internal fact IDs; the module replay supplies that separate evidence. Reports are tagged and never overwrite earlier failures.
